Brent Cross Cricklewood: Inside Barnet’s Mixed-Use Mega-Regeneration and the 2026 Capital Stack

The Brent Cross Cricklewood masterplan in Barnet is one of Greater London’s most active mixed-use regenerations. 7,500 homes planned, £8 billion-plus indicative GDV, and the Brent Cross West Thameslink station that opened in late 2022 has done the heavy lifting on the residential GDV anchor.

What’s anchored in 2026

Phase one residential is in delivery. Phase two has secured infrastructure cost resolution and is moving into senior-debt commitment conversations through Q2 2026.

The Thameslink station means residential product clears £700 to £850 per square foot — comfortably above the £650 viability threshold. The shopping-centre transformation provides ground-floor amenity that further supports residential pricing.

Capital structures placed

Senior development finance at 6.5 to 7.0% per annum, 65% LTGDV. Mezzanine layers in at 12% to 90% LTC. BTR forward funding at 5.5 to 5.75% net yields takes the structure further on the residential phases.

JV equity providers are accepting 20 to 24% IRR targets, with the masterplan partner alignment removing meaningful planning and delivery risk.

Adjacent corridors

The Brent Cross rerating reaches Hendon, Wembley, and the wider Barnet commuter belt. The Thameslink corridor pulls demand from elsewhere in north-west London where rail commute times to King’s Cross / Farringdon now look competitive.
